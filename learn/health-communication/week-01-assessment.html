Health Communication Week 1 — Assessment Blueprint

Curated assessment architecture, question-bank map and authentic-task rubric for Foundations of Health Communication.

Week 1 assessment blueprint

This page is an instructor-facing assessment specification for Foundations of Health Communication. It establishes the quality standard that should be used when the remaining Health Communication banks are rewritten.

Assessment purpose

Week 1 should not reward memorising isolated definitions. A prepared learner should be able to diagnose a communication problem, distinguish communication from programme constraints, select defensible objectives, interpret audience response and judge whether a communication claim is supported by evidence.

The redesigned bank contains 30 genuinely distinct items rather than multiple paraphrases of the same definition.

Pool Items Intended use
Practice 8 Formative learning with immediate explanatory feedback and repeat attempts
Mastery 16 Weekly graded quiz; scenario-based application and interpretation
Institutional 6 Protected seed pool for later formal assessment; do not expose in routine practice

Important
The six institutional items are a seed pool, not yet a sufficient stand-alone high-stakes examination bank. Expand and independently review this pool before consequential institutional use.

Recommended Moodle configuration

Practice

  • Use all 8 practice items or allow free navigation through them.
  • Unlimited or generous time.
  • Multiple attempts.
  • Immediate option-level feedback.
  • Do not include practice performance in the formal course grade unless the institution deliberately chooses participation credit.

Mastery

  • Randomly draw 10 of the 16 mastery items.
  • Pilot time limit: 12 minutes because these are scenario-reading items rather than rapid definition recall.
  • One question per page.
  • Shuffle answer options.
  • One scored attempt in mastery mode; a second learning attempt can be institution-configurable.
  • Analyse response time and discrimination before tightening or extending timing.

Institutional assessment

  • Keep this category hidden from student practice.
  • Do not publish the item text on the public LIPPS site.
  • Add more independently reviewed items before using the category for a consequential examination.
  • Delay answer feedback until the assessment window closes.

Learning-outcome map

The Week 1 outcomes are coded as follows:

  • LO1: distinguish health communication from dissemination, publicity and information transfer alone;
  • LO2: analyse communication as a transactional process involving source, message, channel, audience, context, feedback and noise;
  • LO3: distinguish communication objectives from programme activities and distal health outcomes;
  • LO4: select and combine interpersonal, organisational, community, mass and digital levels appropriately;
  • LO5: judge communication against accessibility, actionability, relevance, timeliness and understandability;
  • LO6: explain how trust, culture, language, prior experience and social context shape interpretation;
  • LO7: identify why accurate communication may fail and avoid blaming audiences for non-communication barriers;
  • LO8: perform a basic communication diagnosis and identify evidence needed before claiming success.

Authentic task · Communication Diagnosis Brief

Task: Give students a short public-health scenario containing a mixture of message, channel, trust, audience and service-delivery problems. In 800–1,000 words, students prepare a diagnostic brief that answers:

  1. What is the health problem?
  2. What is the communication problem — and what is not primarily a communication problem?
  3. Who is the primary audience? Who enables or constrains the decision?
  4. What specific decision or action should communication support?
  5. Where are the likely weaknesses in source, message, channel, context, feedback or trust?
  6. Which WHO communication quality dimensions are most threatened?
  7. What one communication objective would be appropriate?
  8. What evidence would you collect before claiming the communication worked?

Students should be rewarded for diagnosis before solution. A polished poster proposal with weak problem analysis should not score highly.

Rubric — 20 marks

Criterion 4 — Excellent 3 — Good 2 — Developing 1 — Weak 0 — Missing / unsafe
Problem diagnosis Clearly separates the health problem, communication problem and non-communication constraints; prioritises them logically. Mostly separates the problems with minor ambiguity. Identifies several issues but mixes communication and programme barriers. Treats the problem mainly as “lack of awareness” without evidence. No meaningful diagnosis or makes a clearly unsafe interpretation.
Audience + decision analysis Identifies primary and supporting audiences, decision roles and contextual constraints with a clear rationale. Correct audiences and decision identified but analysis is incomplete. Audience is broadly identified but decision roles are vague. Audience is defined only demographically or without relation to a decision. No defensible audience or decision identified.
Communication-process reasoning Correctly analyses source, message, channel, context, feedback, trust and noise, focusing on the elements that matter most. Analyses most relevant elements accurately. Lists elements but provides limited interpretation. Uses communication terms superficially or incorrectly. No usable process analysis.
Objective + proposed response Provides a specific measurable communication objective and a response logically matched to the diagnosis; acknowledges what communication cannot solve. Objective and response are generally appropriate with minor gaps. Objective or response is partly appropriate but generic. Response jumps to a channel/product without a defensible objective. Proposed response is irrelevant, misleading or potentially harmful.
Evidence + judgement Specifies appropriate indicators for reach/understanding/trust/action and states limits on causal claims; reasoning is concise and evidence-aware. Identifies useful indicators and mostly cautious interpretation. Mentions measurement but does not clearly link evidence to claims. Relies mainly on outputs such as posters printed or views. No evaluation logic or makes unsupported causal claims.

Suggested pass standard: 10/20, with no score of 0 in Problem diagnosis or Evidence + judgement. Institutions can adjust this threshold to their regulations.

Item-writing standard established by Week 1

Every future mastery or institutional question should, where possible:

  1. begin with a plausible public-health decision or communication situation;
  2. require application, analysis or evaluation rather than term recognition alone;
  3. use distractors that reflect realistic reasoning errors;
  4. have one defensibly best answer;
  5. give explanatory feedback that teaches the reasoning, not merely labels the answer;
  6. avoid testing trivia that could be answered without understanding the communication problem;
  7. distinguish communication failure from service, policy or access failure; and
  8. avoid overclaiming causation from reach, recall or simple before/after observations.
