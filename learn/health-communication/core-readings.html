Health Communication — Core Readings

Core frameworks and readings supporting the 12-week LIPPS Health Communication course.

Core readings

LIPPS Health Communication uses a deliberately mixed evidence base: strategic communication, behavioural science, health literacy, community engagement, emergency communication and programme planning.

Core public-health frameworks

  1. World Health Organization. WHO principles for effective communications. The framework organizes communication around six qualities: accessible, actionable, credible, relevant, timely and understandable.
    https://www.who.int/about/communications/principles

  2. World Health Organization. WHO competency framework, risk communication and community engagement: for stronger and more inclusive health emergency programmes. 2024.
    https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240092501

  3. World Health Organization. WHO competency framework: Building a response workforce to manage infodemics. 2021.
    https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240035287

  4. World Health Organization. Health literacy. Use alongside Week 3 to connect personal capabilities with organizational responsibility for accessible, understandable and usable information.
    https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/health-literacy

  5. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. The CDC Clear Communication Index. Use as a practical material-development and review tool, particularly in Week 6.
    https://www.cdc.gov/ccindex/index.html

  6. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. Crisis & Emergency Risk Communication (CERC) Manual. Use alongside Weeks 5 and 9.
    https://www.cdc.gov/cerc/php/cerc-manual/index.html

  7. National Cancer Institute. Making Health Communication Programs Work (Pink Book). A programme-planning reference covering strategy development, testing, implementation and evaluation.
    https://www.cancer.gov/publications/health-communication

Behavioural-theory backbone

Week 2 introduces the Health Belief Model, Theory of Planned Behaviour, Social Cognitive Theory, social-ecological thinking, COM-B and diffusion of innovations as diagnostic tools rather than competing slogans. In institutional delivery, lecturers should assign the theory readings used by their programme and require students to justify why a selected theory fits the specific determinant and context.

Reading use by week

Week Core emphasis
1 WHO communication principles; NCI programme-planning orientation
2 Behavioural theory and COM-B
3 WHO health literacy; audience insight
4 Person-centred communication, teach-back and informed decisions
5 WHO RCCE competency framework
6 CDC Clear Communication Index; NCI message/material testing
7 NCI channel and programme planning
8 Digital accessibility, privacy and platform metrics
9 WHO RCCE + CDC CERC
10 WHO infodemic-management competency framework
11 NCI programme planning and evaluation
12 WHO accessibility/credibility principles plus institution-specific ethics and data-governance guidance

Source rule
LIPPS examples and synthesis frameworks are teaching devices. They should not be presented as substitutes for current WHO, ministry, professional or institutional guidance when a learner is making an actual public-health decision.

Authoring and evidence update — v2.0

The expanded lessons use these sources as framework anchors, not as scripts to reproduce verbatim:

  • World Health Organization. Principles for effective communications: accessible, actionable, credible, relevant, timely and understandable. https://www.who.int/about/communications/principles
  • World Health Organization. RCCE competency framework (2024), emphasizing community-centred emergency communication before, during and after emergencies. https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240092501
  • World Health Organization. Infodemic management resources, including listening to community concerns, improving risk understanding, building resilience to misinformation and engaging communities. https://www.who.int/health-topics/infodemic
  • U.S. CDC. Clear Communication Index, a research-based tool for developing and assessing public communication materials. https://www.cdc.gov/ccindex/
  • U.S. CDC. Crisis & Emergency Risk Communication Manual. https://www.cdc.gov/cerc/php/cerc-manual/index.html
  • U.S. National Cancer Institute. Making Health Communication Programs Work and Making Data Talk. https://www.cancer.gov/publications/health-communication

LIPPS examples, synthesis, assessment scenarios and SIGNAL framework remain original instructional material.
