LIPPS LEARN · 12 WEEKS

Health Communication

Communicate so that information can be understood, trusted and used.

This applied course connects communication theory to real public-health decisions. Learners move from communication fundamentals and behavioural theory through audience insight, clinical communication, community engagement, message design, media strategy, digital communication, risk communication, infodemic management, evaluation and ethics.

12 weeks Applied cases Timed objective quizzes Moodle-ready

Course philosophy

LIPPS Health Communication is built around one principle:

Communication succeeds only when the intended audience can access it, interpret it, trust it and use it in a real decision.

The course therefore treats communication as a public-health intervention that must be diagnosed, designed, implemented and evaluated—not as the production of posters or social-media content.

By the end of the course, learners should be able to

  • diagnose health communication problems before selecting messages or channels;
  • use behavioural and communication theory to explain determinants of action;
  • analyse audiences, culture, health literacy, accessibility and communication environments;
  • conduct effective interpersonal and clinical communication;
  • design participatory community engagement and feedback systems;
  • develop and pre-test clear, actionable messages;
  • select integrated channel and media strategies;
  • design safer, more equitable digital health communication;
  • communicate uncertainty and risk during public-health emergencies;
  • identify and manage misinformation, disinformation and infodemic problems;
  • design and evaluate health communication campaigns; and
  • apply ethical, equity and AI-governance principles to health communication.

The 12-week course

01

Foundations of Health Communication

Explain health communication as a strategic public-health process that connects evidence, audiences, messages, channels, feedback and action.

Mastery: 10 timed scenario questions sampled from a curated 30-item bank.
02

Communication and Behaviour-Change Theory

Use behavioural and communication theory to diagnose why a health behaviour occurs and to select plausible communication and non-communication responses.

Mastery: 10 timed scenario questions sampled from a curated 30-item bank.
03

Audience Analysis, Culture and Health Literacy

Use formative audience insight, health-literacy principles and cultural context to design communication that people can access, understand and use.

Mastery: 10 timed scenario questions sampled from a curated 30-item bank.
04

Interpersonal and Clinical Communication

Use patient-centred interpersonal communication to elicit concerns, explain information clearly, check understanding and support informed decisions.

Mastery: 10 timed scenario questions sampled from a curated 30-item bank.
05

Community Engagement and Participatory Communication

Design community engagement as a two-way, accountable process that shares information, listens, adapts interventions and builds workable solutions with affected communities.

Mastery: 10 timed scenario questions sampled from a curated 30-item bank.
06

Message Development and Pre-testing

Develop clear, actionable health messages from defined objectives and improve them through systematic pre-testing with intended audiences.

Mastery: 10 timed scenario questions sampled from a curated 30-item bank.
07

Communication Channels and Media Strategy

Select and combine communication channels and messengers according to audience access, trust, objective, reach, frequency, interactivity, cost and decision timing.

Mastery: 10 timed scenario questions sampled from a curated 30-item bank.
08

Digital Health and Social Media Communication

Design and evaluate digital health communication while accounting for platform dynamics, privacy, accessibility, digital exclusion and the difference between engagement metrics and health outcomes.

Mastery: 10 timed scenario questions sampled from a curated 30-item bank.
09

Risk and Crisis Communication

Communicate rapidly and transparently during health threats while acknowledging uncertainty, supporting protective action, listening to affected communities and adapting as evidence changes.

Mastery: 10 timed scenario questions sampled from a curated 30-item bank.
10

Misinformation, Disinformation and Infodemic Management

Identify harmful information problems, prioritise them by likely health impact and respond through listening, accurate communication, trusted messengers, community engagement and resilience-building.

Mastery: 10 timed scenario questions sampled from a curated 30-item bank.
11

Communication Campaign Design and Evaluation

Design a coherent health communication campaign from problem diagnosis through objectives, strategy, implementation, monitoring and evaluation.

Mastery: 10 timed scenario questions sampled from a curated 30-item bank.
12

Ethics, Equity and the Future of Health Communication

Evaluate health communication for ethical integrity, equity, accessibility and responsible use of emerging technologies, then integrate the full course using the LIPPS SIGNAL framework.

Mastery: 10 timed scenario questions sampled from a curated 30-item bank.

How every week works

ORIENT — begin with a realistic public-health communication problem.
LEARN — build concepts and decision frameworks.
APPLY — work through an integrated case.
PRACTISE — answer untimed reasoning questions with feedback in Moodle.
DEMONSTRATE — complete a short timed mastery quiz drawn from a larger question bank.

The weekly quizzes are designed to be AI-resilient rather than “AI-proof.” Scenario-based items, random banks, shuffled options and calibrated time limits make knowing the material the most efficient strategy while preserving normal accessibility and approved accommodations.

Source backbone

The course draws on established health-communication and behavioural-science literature, with particular emphasis on the WHO Strategic Communications Framework, WHO Risk Communication and Community Engagement guidance and competency framework, WHO infodemic-management resources, behavioural theories commonly used in health promotion, and programme-planning principles from Making Health Communication Programs Work.

LIPPS adds original instructional syntheses, African public-health scenarios, structured decision problems and the LIPPS SIGNAL capstone framework.

Core readings and pilot status

The course is suitable for a university teaching pilot, but the current Moodle banks should not yet be used as a high-stakes examination bank. Many foundation items are useful for practice; mastery items must be separated, rewritten toward novel scenarios and then piloted.
