Epidemiology II

Advanced Epidemiologic Methods and Causal Inference

Move from identifying bias to specifying estimands, identifying causal effects, fitting defensible models and testing how conclusions change when assumptions are weakened.

MSc Epidemiology PhD preparation R laboratories Causal inference

Entry standard

This course assumes competence in disease-frequency measures, cohort/case-control/cross-sectional designs, bias and confounding, basic regression and R. It is designed to follow Epidemiology I, not replace it.

The goal is not to turn causal inference into a collection of recipes. The learner should be able to state the target estimand, identify the assumptions required to estimate it, choose an analysis that corresponds to that estimand, and explain how violations would affect interpretation.

Assessment

The recommended model is 15% advanced laboratory portfolio, 20% DAG/identification assignment, 25% reproducible causal analysis report, 20% target-trial protocol and 20% methods viva. A learner should not pass the course by MCQ performance alone.
